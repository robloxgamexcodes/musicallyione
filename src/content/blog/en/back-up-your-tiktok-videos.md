If you post on TikTok, your videos live inside one app, on one account. Accounts get locked, videos get removed by mistake, and phones get lost. A backup takes a few minutes and means your work is not tied to a single platform.

This guide covers three practical ways to back up videos **you made yourself**, and what each one gives you. Menu names inside TikTok change from time to time, so if a label looks slightly different, look for the closest match.

> **Quick answer:** Keep your original files from the day you film them. For videos already posted, save them from the app, request your data export from TikTok, and use a browser tool for public posts as a spare copy.

---

## Way 1: Save the video from inside TikTok

The simplest option is TikTok's own save feature.

1. Open your video in the app.
2. Tap **Share**.
3. Choose **Save video**, if it is offered.

Two things to know. First, the saved copy normally carries the TikTok logo and your username. That is fine for an archive but not ideal for reposting elsewhere. Second, creators can restrict downloads on their videos in their privacy settings, so check that setting if the option is missing on your own post.

## Way 2: Request your data from TikTok

TikTok lets you ask for a copy of the information in your account. In the app, open **Settings and privacy**, then look under **Account** for the option to **download your data**. You can usually choose a file format and request the export, then download it when it is ready.

What you get depends on what TikTok includes at the time, and the export can take a while to be prepared. Treat it as a record of your account activity and a second safety net, not as your only copy of the videos themselves.

## The best backup is the original file

Whatever you do after posting, the highest quality always comes from the file you made **before** uploading. TikTok re-compresses uploads, so every copy you save later starts from a slightly reduced version.

Good habits:

- Keep the **raw clips and the exported edit** in a folder on your phone or computer.
- Copy that folder to a cloud drive or an external disk.
- Name files with a date and a short title so you can find them later.
- If you edit in a separate app, save the **project file** too.

## A simple backup routine

| How often | What to do |
|---|---|
| After each post | Keep the exported file in your "TikTok originals" folder |
| Monthly | Copy that folder to cloud storage or a disk |
| Every few months | Request a fresh data export from TikTok |
| Before big changes | Back up first if you plan to switch accounts, change your username or delete videos |

## Permission and responsible use

Backing up **your own** videos is straightforward. Videos made by other people belong to them, and downloading them does not give you the right to repost them as your own. Save others' clips for personal reference at most, ask before you reuse them, and credit the creator when you have permission. Many sounds on TikTok are also copyrighted music, so check usage rights before publishing anything that includes them. This is general information, not legal advice.

## FAQ

### Will a backup include my comments and likes?
That depends on what TikTok includes in its data export. A video file saved from the app or a browser tool contains only the video and its sound.

### Does saving a video change it?
Saving does not alter the original on TikTok, but the saved copy reflects TikTok's compression, so it will not be as sharp as your original file.

### Can I back up private videos with MusicallyDown?
No. Browser tools only work with public videos. For private posts, use TikTok's own save option or your original files.

### How do I keep a video without the watermark?
Use the original you exported before uploading, or a no-watermark option when one is available for a public video.
