Android has several free, open-source tools that can help, but they take different approaches. This guide compares **Seal**, **YTDLnis** and **NewPipe** so you can choose the one that fits your habits.

> **Use downloads responsibly:** Download only videos you own, content under an open licence, or material you have permission to save. Downloading copyrighted content without permission may infringe copyright or breach a platform's terms. Rules vary by country.

## Seal: a polished all-rounder

[Seal](https://github.com/JunkFood02/Seal) is an Android video and audio downloader built on yt-dlp. Its Material You interface aims to make common tasks straightforward while still offering advanced controls.

### What Seal does well

- **Simple workflow:** paste a link, review the available options and start a download.
- **Video and audio options:** quality, audio-only downloads, subtitles and thumbnails may be available depending on the source and version.
- **Playlists and metadata:** playlist downloads and metadata or thumbnail handling are among its useful features.
- **Advanced controls:** custom yt-dlp command templates and aria2c integration may suit experienced users.
- **Open source:** the project uses GPLv3 and publishes releases through its official channels.

### Seal's limitations

- Download concurrency and other details may change by release; verify current behavior instead of relying on older tests.
- It is not distributed through the usual Google Play listing, so installation takes extra care.
- It depends on yt-dlp. A website change can break downloads until the relevant component is updated.

**Best for:** People who want a clean interface without giving up useful download controls.

## YTDLnis: for queues and batch downloads

YTDLnis is another Android app built around yt-dlp. It focuses on download management and gives users more control over queues and batch tasks.

### What YTDLnis does well

- **Batch-oriented workflow:** useful when saving multiple videos or playlists you have permission to keep.
- **Customization:** format choices and advanced options can help users familiar with yt-dlp.
- **Broad potential site support:** it benefits from yt-dlp's supported extractors, although individual sites can stop working.

### YTDLnis limitations

- Its larger set of controls may take longer for beginners to learn.
- Like Seal, it depends on yt-dlp and can be affected when websites change their delivery systems.
- Check the current repository for the licence, supported Android versions and exact feature availability before installing.

**Best for:** Experienced users who manage larger download queues and want more control.

## NewPipe: a lightweight streaming app

NewPipe takes a different approach. It is primarily an alternative front end for supported streaming services, with downloads as one feature. It does not use yt-dlp.

### What NewPipe does well

- **Watch and save in one place:** browse supported services, stream, play audio in the background and download where available.
- **Lightweight design:** it avoids Google services and can be useful on modest devices.
- **Straightforward controls:** everyday actions generally do not require command templates or external downloader configuration.

### NewPipe limitations

- It supports fewer services than apps using yt-dlp.
- It offers fewer advanced download and post-processing controls.
- Platform changes can temporarily break playback or downloads until the project releases a fix.

**Best for:** People who mainly stream and only occasionally save supported content for offline use.

## Which app should you choose?

- **Choose Seal** for a good balance of usability and download features.
- **Choose YTDLnis** if queue management and batch downloads matter most.
- **Choose NewPipe** if you want a lightweight streaming experience with downloads included.

There is no universal winner. Your best choice depends on your device, preferred workflow and the services you use. Check recent release notes before deciding.

## How to install these apps safely

1. **Use an official source.** Start with the project's official repository or its verified F-Droid listing, where available.
2. **Read the release information.** Confirm Android requirements, known issues and the latest supported version.
3. **Avoid random APK mirrors.** A file using a familiar app name is not necessarily authentic.
4. **Review Android's install permission.** Allow installation only for the trusted app you are using, then disable that permission again if it is no longer needed.
5. **Check permissions and prompts.** Be wary of requests unrelated to downloading, and never enter your TikTok, Google or other account password into an unfamiliar prompt.
6. **Test carefully.** Start with content you own or have permission to save.

Open-source code is helpful, but it does not automatically make every redistributed APK trustworthy.

## Limitations to understand

- **Downloads may stop working:** supported websites change, and apps or extraction components may need updates.
- **Restricted content may not work:** do not attempt to bypass logins, paywalls, privacy settings or other access controls.
- **Advanced settings add complexity:** custom commands and external downloader integrations are useful only if you understand them.
- **Files use storage:** high-resolution video and large playlists can take up substantial space. Subtitles, merging and metadata processing may add time.

## Are Seal, YTDLnis and NewPipe safe and private?

All three are open-source projects, which allows code and project activity to be reviewed. That is a positive signal, not a guarantee of security or privacy. Apps still connect to online services and depend on their update channels and third-party infrastructure.

Install from official project channels, keep the app updated, review permissions, and avoid entering account credentials into unexpected dialogs. For the strongest assessment, check the project's current documentation, issue tracker and release history rather than relying on a blanket claim that any app is completely safe.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Seal free?

Seal is free and open source under GPLv3 according to its project information. Check the official repository for current release and licence details.

### Is Seal better than NewPipe?

It depends on your needs. Seal focuses on downloads and uses yt-dlp for broad extractor support. NewPipe combines streaming and downloads in a lightweight app, but supports fewer services and has fewer advanced controls.

### What is the difference between Seal and YTDLnis?

Both use yt-dlp. Seal emphasizes a simpler download workflow, while YTDLnis focuses more on queues and batch management. Details can change between versions.

### Can Seal download from sites other than YouTube?

It may work with many sites supported by yt-dlp. Support depends on the site, the extractor and current versions; it is not guaranteed for every link.

### Is it legal to download videos with these apps?

The apps themselves are software tools, but the legality of a particular download depends on copyright, permissions, platform terms and local law. Save content you own or have permission to download.

### Why have my downloads stopped working?

A website may have changed its systems, or the app or extraction component may be out of date. Check for updates and consult the project's official issue tracker if the problem continues.

### Are these apps safe to install?

Use only verified project releases or trusted F-Droid listings, check permissions and avoid unofficial APK mirrors. Open source does not guarantee that every copy found online is authentic or safe.

## The verdict

**Seal is a sensible starting point for most people** who want a dedicated downloader with a polished interface. **YTDLnis** is worth considering for heavier queue-based workflows, while **NewPipe** is a better fit for people who primarily stream and want downloads as an extra feature.

When a service provides an official offline option, consider using it. Whichever tool you choose, respect creators' rights and download only content you are allowed to keep.
