A song sticks in your head, the video scrolls past, and you cannot remember anything about it. Finding it is usually quick if you know where to look. Here are five methods, ordered from the fastest to the most thorough.

> **Quick answer:** Tap the sound name at the bottom of the video first. If that does not name the song, check the caption and comments, then use a music-recognition app on the audio.

---

## Method 1: Tap the sound name on the video

Every TikTok with audio shows a sound label near the bottom of the screen, usually with a small music icon and a scrolling name. Tap it to open the **sound page**.

- If the sound is an uploaded song, you will often see the **track title and artist** there.
- The page also lists other videos using the same sound, which helps you confirm you have the right one.
- You can add the sound to your favorites to find it again later.

If the label says "original sound" followed by a username, it is audio recorded by the creator. That could be a voiceover, a remix or an edited clip, so it will not always have a song title.

## Method 2: Read the caption and the comments

Creators often credit the music themselves. Check the caption for a song name, and scroll the comments, where viewers frequently ask "what is the song?" and others reply. Sorting or searching the comments for "song" or "name" can save time.

## Method 3: Use a music-recognition app

Play the video aloud near your phone and let a recognition service listen. Common options are Shazam (also built into the iPhone through Siri) and the song-identification features on Android phones and in search apps.

For best results:

- Turn the volume up and reduce background noise.
- Pick a part of the video where the music is clearest, not where someone is talking over it.
- Try a second time if the first attempt fails.

Very short clips, heavily edited remixes and slowed or sped-up versions can be hard to match. Recognition may identify the original song instead of the version you heard.

## Method 4: Save the clip and listen in your own time

If the audio is only a few seconds long, it helps to keep it so you can replay it as many times as you need. For a public video, you can paste the link into the [TikTok to MP3 converter](https://musicaldown.se/tiktok-mp3/) and save the sound as a standalone audio file when an audio option is offered.

Then you can:

- Play the clip repeatedly and hum or type a few lyrics into a search engine.
- Run it through a recognition app without the video in the way.

Keep expectations realistic. The audio you save is only what the video contains, so it may be a short excerpt rather than the full track. Quality also matches the original post.

## Method 5: Search the lyrics or ask a community

If you can make out any words, put them in quotation marks in a search engine along with "TikTok". If that fails, post a short description or the video link on a music-identification community. Mention the timestamp of the part you mean and any details you remember: genre, language, tempo, voice type.

## After you identify the song

Once you have a name and artist, find the **full version through a licensed music service**. This supports the artist and gives you the complete, higher-quality recording instead of a short clip.

A saved TikTok sound is fine for personal listening and for identifying a track. If you plan to use music in your own videos or anything you publish, check the usage rights first. A lot of music on TikTok is copyrighted, and being able to save a file is different from having permission to use it. This is general information, not legal advice.

## FAQ

### Why does the sound say "original sound"?
It means the audio was recorded or uploaded by the creator, not taken from TikTok's music library. It may be speech, an edit or a remix.

### Can I download a full song from a TikTok?
Not necessarily. Many TikTok sounds are short excerpts or edits, so you may only get that portion. Use it to identify the song, then look for the full track on a licensed service.

### Will the saved audio sound better than the video?
No. A converter cannot improve audio quality. You get what the original post contained.

### What if no method finds the song?
Some sounds are unreleased, custom edits or sampled mixes. Asking in the comments or a music community is often your best chance.
