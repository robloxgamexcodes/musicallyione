In July 2026, the US Justice Department concluded that a federal law banning TikTok from government devices no longer applies to the version of the app available in the United States. The reasoning rests on one fact: TikTok's American operations now run through a joint venture, not under ByteDance's control.

This article summarizes the reporting by Joe Walsh at CBS News, last updated July 21, 2026, and explains what the decision does and does not change.

> **Quick summary:** The DOJ's Office of Legal Counsel issued a 12-page opinion saying the 2022 federal-device ban covered only the version of TikTok with ByteDance's ownership structure. Individual agencies can still decide for themselves whether to allow the app. Separate questions about the ownership deal, including a pending lawsuit, remain open.

## What the DOJ decided

The Justice Department's Office of Legal Counsel, which gives legal advice to the executive branch, issued a written opinion about six months after TikTok's US operations moved into a new joint venture made up mostly of American investors. The opinion was addressed to the deputy counsel to the president and runs 12 pages.

Its conclusion is that the federal-device ban no longer reaches the version of TikTok now offered in the US. According to the opinion, Congress targeted only the version of the app that carried ByteDance's ownership features, which the DOJ says no longer describe the current platform.

In a statement to CBS News, the Justice Department said the change follows the plain text of the law and the fact that TikTok's US operations were restructured. In its view, ByteDance no longer controls the platform, and a majority American-owned venture runs it.

---

## Background of the federal device ban

In late 2022, Congress passed bipartisan legislation requiring executive branch agencies to remove TikTok from federal devices. The law also covered any successor application or service developed or provided by ByteDance or an entity it owns.

The motivation was national security. Officials worried that data collected by a ByteDance-owned app could reach the Chinese government. TikTok long said that concern was unfounded.

In 2024, Congress went further and passed a law that would effectively ban TikTok nationwide unless ByteDance divested its US operations by January 2025. The law took effect the day before President Trump's inauguration. Trump, who had supported a ban during his first term but later opposed it, directed the Justice Department not to enforce it while he worked on a deal to shift ownership.

---

## Why the DOJ says the law no longer applies

The argument is about definitions. The 2022 law applied to TikTok and to successors tied to ByteDance. The DOJ reads that language as describing an app under ByteDance's control. Since the joint venture now operates the US version, the department says the statute no longer fits.

ByteDance still holds a minority stake of 19.9%, which sits just under the 20% cap in the 2024 law. The DOJ's position is that a minority stake is not the same as control.

Critics of the deal may read the same facts differently, and the opinion does not settle every legal question. It is an interpretation from within the executive branch, not a ruling from a court.

## What happens next for federal agencies

The opinion does not force any agency to allow TikTok. Each agency can still decide on its own whether to permit downloads on government devices, and it can ban the app for workforce management reasons, such as protecting employee productivity.

According to the opinion, employees of executive branch agencies have been told they may download TikTok onto official devices, subject to each agency's discretion and workplace policies. In practice, rules could vary from one agency to the next.

---

## How the ownership deal got here

A deal that emerged in 2025 was finalized in January 2026. A group of mostly US-based investors took a majority stake in the version of TikTok available to Americans, with ByteDance keeping 19.9%.

One of the new investors is Oracle, chaired by Larry Ellison. His son, David Ellison, is CEO of Paramount, a Skydance corporation and the parent company of CBS News. CBS News disclosed this connection in its own report.

The venture is called TikTok U.S. Data Security, or TikTok USDS. It promised strong cybersecurity controls, including retraining the recommendation algorithm on data from American users. Oracle said it would review and validate the source code on an ongoing basis.

---

## Questions and legal challenges that remain

The deal has drawn scrutiny. Some lawmakers pressed for evidence that the arrangement addresses the national security concerns that led Congress to pass a ban in the first place.

There is also litigation. Two investors in competing tech companies, Alphabet and Meta, sued the federal government, arguing that the deal did not comply with the law. The government has asked for the case to be dismissed, and it was still pending at the time of the July 2026 reporting. CBS News also said it had asked TikTok for comment.

Because of this, the DOJ opinion should be read as one step in an ongoing legal and political debate, not the final word.

## What this means for TikTok users and marketers

For most everyday users, nothing changes. The opinion concerns official government devices, not personal phones.

For marketers, the signal is more about stability. Each ruling that treats the US version of TikTok as a separate, American-run service reduces one type of regulatory uncertainty. It does not eliminate the risk from pending litigation or from future political shifts. Brands that rely heavily on TikTok should still keep a diversified channel mix and watch for legal updates.

Government communicators, such as agency press offices, may now have more room to use the platform, depending on their own agency's policy.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is TikTok now allowed on all federal devices?**
Not automatically. The DOJ says the 2022 law no longer bars it, but each agency can still decide whether to permit it.

**Who issued the opinion?**
The Justice Department's Office of Legal Counsel, in a 12-page document addressed to the deputy counsel to the president.

**Why did the DOJ change its view?**
It says TikTok's US operations are now run by a majority American-owned joint venture, and ByteDance no longer controls the platform.

**How much of TikTok US does ByteDance own?**
19.9%, just under the 20% limit in the 2024 law.

**Is the ownership deal still being challenged?**
Yes. A lawsuit from two investors in Alphabet and Meta is still pending, and the government has asked for it to be dismissed.

## The bottom line

The DOJ's opinion removes one federal restriction on TikTok by arguing that the US version is no longer the ByteDance-controlled app Congress targeted. Agencies keep the freedom to set their own rules, and the broader questions about the ownership deal are not resolved. Anyone relying on TikTok for communication or marketing should treat the situation as improved but not settled.
