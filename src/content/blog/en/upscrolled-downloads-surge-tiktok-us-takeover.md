When TikTok's American joint venture was finalized in late January 2026, many users started asking where else they could post and watch videos. One small app picked up most of that attention: **UpScrolled**. Within days it reached second place in the App Store's social networking category, and its servers struggled to keep up.

This article summarizes the reporting by Aisha Malik at TechCrunch from January 26, 2026, and looks at what the numbers do and do not tell us.

> **Quick summary:** UpScrolled, a social app that promises political neutrality and no shadowbanning, saw daily downloads rise about 2,850% after the TikTok US deal closed. It reached roughly 140,000 lifetime downloads, 75,000 of them in the US. The jump is real, but it started from a very small base, and long-term retention is still unknown.

## What changed at TikTok in the US

TikTok announced a deal with a group of non-Chinese investors to form a majority American-owned company that keeps the app running in the United States. ByteDance, the Chinese parent, holds less than 20% of the new entity. Three managing investors (Oracle, Silver Lake, and Abu Dhabi-based MGX) each hold 15%.

The structure was designed to keep the app available, but it also gave some users a reason to reconsider. Two concerns drove most of the conversation:

- **Content moderation:** Some users, including Senator Chris Murphy and singer Billie Eilish, said posts critical of ICE appeared to be suppressed. Others said they could not search for information about protests in Minneapolis. TikTok attributed the glitches to a power outage at a US data center.
- **Privacy:** An updated privacy policy allows the app to track GPS coordinates, among other data, which led some people to encourage deleting the app.

These censorship claims were not independently confirmed in the reporting, so they should be treated as allegations. Even so, user perception can move download numbers as much as verified facts do.

## What is UpScrolled

UpScrolled is a social network that blends features from Instagram and X. People can share photos, videos, and text posts, discover new content, and send direct messages. It is available on iOS and Android.

The founder is Issam Hijazi, a Palestinian-Jordanian-Australian technologist who started the project in 2025. The platform's central promise is that it will not shadowban anyone and that every post gets a fair chance to be seen. Hijazi describes it as more than an alternative to Meta, X, or TikTok, with creators and communities holding real control, transparency, and accountability.

For users worried about hidden moderation, that message arrived at exactly the right moment.

## UpScrolled download numbers

Market intelligence provider Appfigures supplied the data reported by TechCrunch:

| Metric | Figure |
|---|---|
| Downloads from deal day (Thursday) to Saturday | About 41,000 |
| Share of lifetime installs | Nearly one-third |
| Average daily downloads since the deal | About 14,000 |
| Increase in daily downloads | 2,850% |
| Total lifetime downloads | 140,000 |
| US-based installs | 75,000 |

At the time, UpScrolled ranked 12th overall in Apple's App Store. About 54% of all installs came from the US, which suggests the spike was tied closely to American TikTok users.

A 2,850% increase sounds enormous, but percentages can mislead when the starting point is small. Before the news, the app was averaging only a few hundred downloads a day, so even a modest wave of interest produced a huge percentage gain.

The traffic also caught the team off guard. In a post on X, the company said its servers could not handle the demand and described itself as a tiny team scaling as quickly as possible.

## How UpScrolled compares to Skylight

UpScrolled was not the only beneficiary. Skylight, a TikTok alternative built on open-source technology, reported more than 380,000 sign-ups in the same period, well above UpScrolled's lifetime installs.

The two apps pitch themselves differently:

- **UpScrolled** focuses on neutrality and fair visibility, using a familiar mix of photo, video, and text features.
- **Skylight** relies on open-source foundations, which appeals to people who care about how a platform is built.

Be careful with direct comparisons. Sign-ups and downloads are different measurements, and neither app is close to TikTok's scale.

## Will the growth last

News-driven spikes often fade once headlines move on. Whether UpScrolled keeps its new users depends on a few practical factors:

1. **Stability.** If servers keep failing, new users will not wait around.
2. **Content supply.** People stay where their favorite creators post. An empty feed is the fastest way to lose someone.
3. **Retention.** The reporting covered downloads, not how many people stayed active afterward.
4. **Trust.** A promise of no shadowbanning needs to be backed by transparency about how the feed works.

Remember that this data is from January 2026. Check current rankings and user figures before drawing conclusions about where the app stands today.

## What this means for creators and marketers

The lesson is straightforward: do not depend on one platform. Ownership changes, policy updates, and algorithm shifts can change your reach quickly. Keep a presence on more than one network, and maintain an email list or website that you control.

Early adoption on a new network can pay off because there is less competition for attention. It can also waste time if the audience never arrives. Test with a small amount of effort first, watch engagement, and then decide how much to invest.
